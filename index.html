Self-healing operations

Running · all the time

Automations are only worth something if they run when nobody is watching. This is the scheduler and watchdog under every system on this page. A run that couldn't happen — the machine asleep, no internet — shows as "did not run", not as a failure, and goes as soon as it can. A real failure arrives as a card saying what broke and where, with a "Fix with AI" button that hands that exact error to an AI engineer.

Each night it reads the day's AI news and picks the stories worth a video. Crime, deaths and politics are left out, and links already used are skipped.

The script — about 35 seconds — is written from the article alone. A fact gate refuses any quote or figure that isn't in the source text.

Every scene gets an AI-generated image, animated into a short clip, plus real logos where the story names a company. It all runs on our own GPU, so a video has no per-render cost.

A cloned voice reads the script. Speech-to-text then listens to every line and compares it with the script; a line that doesn't match is flagged and redone before the video moves on.

Scenes, word-by-word captions, music and sound effects are put together into a vertical video. Two photo carousels per story are designed alongside it.

Everything is scheduled across the next day, from 08:00 to 21:30, with captions that explain the story. Views are collected every two hours and feed the content tracker.

A daily scan reads the open campaigns — their budgets, rules and source videos — and picks the ones worth clipping.

The source videos are transcribed and cut into short vertical clips at their strongest moments.

Each clip gets captions, a hook and the disclosure the campaign asks for, following the brief's own words.

Frames and transcript are checked line by line against the campaign's rules and each platform's safety rules. A missing tag is fixed; anything doubtful is held, never posted.

Approved clips are scheduled on TikTok, Instagram and YouTube.

As each post goes live, its link is submitted to the campaign within 30 minutes, so its views start counting toward payment.

Every week it reads the public leaderboards for each kind of model — LMArena, Artificial Analysis, MTEB, Open ASR and others — and the makers' own pricing pages.

A price or score without the page it was read on is dropped. Nothing is guessed or carried over from memory.

Models are ranked only against others of their own kind, on the same board, so a speech model is never weighed against a chatbot.

For each place a model is used, it previews what a switch would change: price, quality, and whether it can run on our own hardware.

A candidate runs on real work next to the model in use and is scored on the same material.

Only a clear win is switched in, and every switch keeps the previous setting one click away.

The day's first analysis runs before the US market opens, then every hour while it trades, and once more after the close.

Drawdowns, moving averages and RSI are computed in code from the raw prices. The AI never produces a figure.

Stocks that fell hard but still look sound are flagged on the dashboard, refreshed every hour the market is open.

Paper portfolios at different risk levels buy and sell by fixed momentum rules, with trailing stops checked every 15 minutes.

The AI reads the computed numbers and writes a short plain-language brief, sent every weekday morning.

Every Saturday a review may change one setting — and keeps the change only if it wins the 3-, 6- and 12-month backtests.

The team drops photos into one shared Google Drive folder. It is checked every 5 minutes; nothing else to learn.

Light, colour and crop are corrected for the post format.

Where it helps, the background is cleaned up or replaced; a photo that is already good is left alone.

Text and layout are added in the brand's fonts and colours.

Each edit is laid next to the original on a review sheet and judged before it can go further.

Approved posts are queued to publish, with their captions.

Every workflow runs on a clock or a watcher: nightly builds, hourly checks, folders and inboxes watched every few minutes.

Heavy jobs — video renders, model runs — take turns on the GPU, so one never starves another.

A watchdog keeps the services up, and AI work that was interrupted mid-answer is picked up again where it stopped.

Runs missed while the machine was asleep or offline are caught up as soon as it's back — shown as "did not run", never as a failure.

A real failure arrives as a card with what broke, where and how; "Fix with AI" hands that exact error to an AI engineer.

Notifications are kept for what matters — a failure, a result, something that needs a person — not for every finished run.
